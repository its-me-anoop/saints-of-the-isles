# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Visitors of varied ages and abilities encounter Saints of the Isles in a museum or church exhibition. They use a shared tablet to explore places across Britain and Ireland while a nearby large display presents the selected saint. The experience must be immediately understandable to first-time visitors, comfortable for touch, and legible from both close range and across a room.

## Product Purpose

Saints of the Isles turns geography into an accessible pilgrimage through the lives of Catholic saints connected to Britain and Ireland. A visitor should be able to find or discover a saint, understand their historical and spiritual significance, and share the reveal with people gathered around the large display. Success means visitors can begin without instruction, remain oriented between the two screens, and leave with curiosity about a person or place.

## Brand Personality

Sacred, contemplative, and scholarly. The voice is reverent without becoming forbidding, informative without feeling academic, and atmospheric without obscuring the content.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS dashboards, theme-park spectacle, kitschy medieval decoration, neon or glowing dark-mode effects, emoji-led controls, identical card grids, glassmorphism, and ornamental motion that competes with reading. The interface must not look like a generic AI-generated product template.

## Design Principles

1. Make the first interaction self-evident for a visitor who has received no instructions.
2. Let historical portraits, places, and words carry the emotional weight.
3. Use reverence through restraint, rhythm, and careful typography rather than decoration.
4. Keep the tablet and large display visibly connected so every action has a clear result.
5. Reveal scholarly depth progressively without overwhelming a casual visitor.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Design for people of varied ages, reading ability, vision, hearing, mobility, and technical confidence. Meet WCAG AA contrast as a baseline, provide large touch targets and visible keyboard focus, honor reduced-motion preferences, never rely on color or audio alone, and keep core exploration usable without narration, music, or fine motor precision.
